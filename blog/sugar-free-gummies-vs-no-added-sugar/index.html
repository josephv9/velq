Sugar-Free Gummies vs. No Added Sugar: How to Read the Label

Sugar-free and no added sugar are not interchangeable. One describes a very low sugar content under US labeling rules; the other concerns sugars added during processing and has additional conditions. A gummy can have no added sugar and still contain naturally occurring sugars. Compare the full Nutrition Facts panel rather than assuming either claim tells you everything about the snack.

By Velq Editorial · General nutrition and label education

What do the two claims actually mean?

US packaged food label terms
Label wordingWhat to check
Sugar-freeUnder FDA rules, the food generally must have less than 0.5 g sugars per labeled serving and reference amount customarily consumed, subject to additional ingredient and calorie-disclosure conditions.
No added sugarA claim about processing, subject to regulatory conditions; naturally occurring sugar may remain.
Reduced sugarA comparative claim. Check the stated reference food and the amount of reduction; the food may still contain sugar.

The FDA's guidance on sugar-free claims explains the conditions beyond the number of grams. These terms do not automatically mean a food is low calorie, appropriate for a particular medical diet, or nutritionally superior to other foods.

Total sugars, added sugars and serving size

Total sugars include naturally occurring and added sugars. Added sugars are listed separately within that total; do not add the two lines together. The FDA's added sugars label explainer describes the difference.

A hypothetical gummy-label example

A label lists 3 g total sugars and 1 g added sugar per five pieces. Two labeled servings contain 6 g total sugars, including 2 g added sugar. These are illustrative numbers, not a real product's nutrition facts.

Check pieces per serving and servings per package before comparing. A larger pouch can have more than one labeled serving. The serving comparison calculator helps you compare documented gummy labels on a consistent basis.

What else should you check in lower-sugar gummies?

Check ingredients for sweeteners, dietary fiber, allergens and any additional active ingredients. Sugar alcohols and certain fibers can affect digestive tolerance, and individual tolerance varies. A product with no added sugar is not necessarily free of sweeteners. If a gummy contains caffeine, compare the declared caffeine amount and follow its directions; it is not ordinary candy intended for children.

A shopping checklist you can use today

  1. Check the exact claim on the front of the package.
  2. Find the complete serving and number of servings.
  3. Compare total sugars, added sugars, calories and total carbohydrate.
  4. Check protein and dietary fiber if those nutrients matter to your purchase.
  5. Read the ingredient list, warnings and storage directions.

Frequently asked questions

Does no added sugar mean zero total sugar?

No. Naturally occurring sugars can still be present; read the total sugars line and ingredient list.

Does sugar-free mean calorie-free?

No. A food may contain calories from other components. Check its calorie information separately.

Are sugar-free gummies automatically a high-fiber snack?

No. Fiber and sugar claims are separate. Compare dietary fiber grams per full serving.
