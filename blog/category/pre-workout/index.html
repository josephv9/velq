Pre-workout snacks

From a quick gym session to a longer run, choose pre-workout snacks that fit your timing, activity and personal tolerance.

EXPLORE THE GUIDES ↓

THE BASICS

Build a pre-workout snack around your session

There is no universal best pre-workout snack. A full meal several hours before training may suit some people, while others prefer a small, familiar snack closer to exercise. For prolonged or demanding workouts, carbohydrates and fluid can be especially important.

01

Match the timing

Choose a meal or a snack based on how soon you plan to exercise. Close to training, avoid trying an unfamiliar product or a portion that tends to upset your stomach.

02

Consider your fuel needs

Carbohydrate-containing foods such as fruit or toast can be useful before exercise. Protein matters across your day; it is not a replacement for carbohydrate fuel during prolonged activity.

03

Account for caffeine

Caffeine is optional, not a requirement for a good workout. Check total intake from coffee, energy drinks and supplements, and consider your sensitivity and bedtime.

GOOD TO KNOW

Frequently asked questions

What are good quick pre-workout snacks?

A banana, toast, or another familiar carbohydrate-containing food can be practical. Your best choice depends on the session, time since your last meal and what you tolerate.

Should I eat high-fiber snacks immediately before training?

Not everyone tolerates a large amount of fiber close to exercise. If it causes discomfort, choose a familiar lower-fiber option before training and get fiber at other times of the day.

Are caffeine gummies a complete pre-workout?

Not necessarily. Caffeine is not a source of carbohydrate fuel or hydration. A caffeinated gummy is also not suitable for everyone, and it should not replace the food and fluids your activity requires.
