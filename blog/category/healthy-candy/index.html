Lower-sugar candy and gummy snacks

Love a sweet snack? Learn how to compare gummy candy and lower-sugar alternatives by the whole nutrition label, not just the number on the front of the pack.

THE BASICS

What to look for in a candy alternative

A lower-sugar snack can still differ widely in serving size, calories, fiber, ingredients and intended use. Compare the amount you would actually eat, rather than assuming that every gummy has the same serving.

01

Check total and added sugars

Read the Nutrition Facts panel. A product with less added sugar is not necessarily sugar-free, and sugar-free does not mean calorie-free.

02

Compare the entire serving

Look at the number of gummies, grams of protein and fiber, total carbohydrate and calories. Compare equivalent portions when possible.

03

Read the ingredients

Check the sweeteners, allergens and any added caffeine. Some fiber ingredients and sweeteners can cause digestive discomfort, particularly in larger amounts.

GOOD TO KNOW

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between low-sugar and sugar-free candy?

They are not interchangeable descriptions. Sugar-free is a regulated labeling claim in the United States; lower-sugar wording may describe a comparison with another product. Read the actual Nutrition Facts and ingredient list.

Is all candy with fiber high in protein?

No. Fiber and protein are different nutrients, and amounts vary by product. Compare the grams of each per serving rather than assuming that a gummy with one contains the other.

Are caffeinated gummies ordinary candy?

No. Check the amount of caffeine per serving and the product's directions. Caffeinated products are not intended for children; choose an appropriate non-caffeinated snack when needed.
