“Not entered” means unknown or not supplied, never zero. Values shown as <0.01 are smaller than the display precision. Product labels are rounded, so derived values are arithmetic estimates rather than laboratory measurements.

How the value math works

Cost per serving = package price ÷ labeled servings
Cost per 10 g protein = serving cost × 10 ÷ protein grams
Cost per 5 g fiber = serving cost × 5 ÷ fiber grams
  1. Enter the price you would actually pay for the whole package. Include shipping or tax consistently for both products, or exclude both.
  2. Use the servings-per-container count for that package and the nutrients for that same serving.
  3. Use the same currency. These results are displayed in USD; the tool does not convert currencies.
  4. After comparing cost, review the protein source, fiber ingredient, allergens, sweeteners, and directions separately.

Cost per 10 g protein or 5 g fiber is a standardized price calculation. It is not a suggested portion or number of servings to take.

A worked example

Fictional product A costs $24 for 12 labeled servings: $24 ÷ 12 = $2 per serving. With 10 g protein and 5 g fiber per serving, each standardized cost is $2.

Fictional product B costs $30 for 30 servings: $30 ÷ 30 = $1 per serving. With 8 g protein and 4 g fiber per serving, the cost per 10 g protein and per 5 g fiber is $1.25 each.

Price alone cannot identify a better product

These formulas do not test nutrient quality, ingredient safety, effectiveness, or suitability. A free package can have a zero cost; a product with zero labeled fiber cannot have a meaningful cost per 5 g fiber. Both cases are handled separately.
